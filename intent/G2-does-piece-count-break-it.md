# G2 — Does piece count break it, or is that a coincidence?

**Status:** open · **Blocked by:** none · **Effort:** ~1 week, data already on Spartan

## Why it matters

The facts we have line up suspiciously with size:

| Material | Pieces | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Breaking Bad / Fractura | 6 or fewer | assembles well |
| Juglet scan | 9 | shape does not close |
| Tray-000 | 40 | one sherd of forty placed correctly (2.5%) |

They also line up with **synthetic versus real**, and with **whole object versus tray of
mixed sherds**. Three explanations, one ordering. Until they are separated, "GARF does not
scale" is a story rather than a finding.

This matters practically: a conservator's tray holds tens of sherds, not six. If piece
count is the wall, the deployment question changes from "which model" to "how do we hand
it the problem in smaller pieces".

## Done when

- [ ] The same material evaluated at several piece counts — subsets of one object, so
      synthetic-versus-real and object-versus-tray are held constant
- [ ] Run-to-run variation reported, so a drop is distinguishable from noise
- [ ] Stated in physical terms: at what tray size does this stop being useful to someone
      actually sorting sherds

## Gate

If accuracy falls off with piece count on **held-constant** material, that is a real
scaling limit and belongs in the thesis. If it does not, the Tray-000 result is about the
tray being real archaeological material rather than large — and the question becomes
[G1](G1-juglet-failure-mechanism.md) again at a different scale.
